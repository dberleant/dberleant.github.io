Microcomputers (personal computers)

[edit]

Microcomputers became the most common type of computer in the late 20th century.By the late 20th century, how did microcomputers compare with other types of computer in popularity?They had become the most common type. The term "microcomputer" was introduced with the advent of systems based on single-chip microprocessors.What development led to the term "microcomputer" being introduced?Systems based on single-chip microprocessors. The best-known[citation needed] early system was the Altair 8800, introduced in 1975.What is given as an example of an early microcomputer system?The Altair 8800, introduced in 1975. The term "microcomputer" has practically become an anachronism as it has fallen into disuse.[1]Why has the term "microcomputer" become an anachronism?It has fallen into disuse.

These computers include:

  • Desktop computers – A case put under or on a desk.How are desktop computers described?As computers in a case that sits under or on a desk. The display may be optional, depending on use.Does a desktop computer always come with its own display?No. The display may be optional, depending on how the computer is used. The case size may vary, depending on the required expansion slots.What causes the case size of a desktop computer to vary?The number of expansion slots required. Very small computers of this kind may be integrated into the monitor.Where might a very small desktop-type computer be built in?Into the monitor.
  • Rackmount computers – The cases of these computers fit into 19-inch racks, and may be space-optimized and very flat.What is distinctive about the cases of rackmount computers?They fit into 19-inch racks and may be space-optimized and very flat. A dedicated display, keyboard, and mouse may not exist, but a KVM switch or built-in remote control (via LAN or other means) can be used to gain console access.How can someone control a rackmount computer that has no dedicated display, keyboard, or mouse?Through a KVM switch or a built-in remote control, such as over a LAN. Many servers are rackmount computers and data centers may have dozens or hundreds of racks with hundreds or thousands of rackmount computers acting as servers.Why are rackmount computers common in data centers?Many servers are rackmount computers, and a data center may hold hundreds or thousands of them acting as servers.
  • In-car computers (carputers) – Built into automobiles, for entertainment, navigation, etc.What are in-car computers built into automobiles used for?Entertainment, navigation, and similar functions.
  • Laptops and notebook computers – Portable and all in one case.What do laptops and notebook computers offer compared with desktops?They are portable, with everything in one case.
  • Tablet computer – Like laptops, but with a touch-screen, entirely replacing the physical keyboard.How does a tablet computer differ from a laptop?It has a touch screen that entirely replaces the physical keyboard.
  • Smartphones, smartbooks, and palmtop computers – Small handheld personal computers with limited hardware specifications.What limits smartphones and palmtop computers compared with other personal computers?Their limited hardware specifications.
  • Programmable calculator – Like small handhelds, but specialized in mathematical work.How does a programmable calculator differ from a small handheld computer?It is specialized for mathematical work.
  • Video game consoles – Fixed computers built specifically for entertainment purposes.What is the defining purpose of video game consoles?They are fixed computers built specifically for entertainment.
  • Handheld game consoles – The same as game consoles, but small and portable.How do handheld game consoles relate to regular game consoles?They are the same but small and portable.

Minicomputers (mid-range computers)

[edit]

Minicomputers (colloquially, minis) are a class of multi-user computers that lie in the middle range of the computing spectrum, in between the smallest mainframe computers and the largest single-user systems (microcomputers or personal computers).Where do minicomputers sit in the computing spectrum?In the middle range, between the smallest mainframes and the largest single-user systems. The term supermini computer or simply supermini was used to distinguish more powerful minicomputers that approached mainframes in capability.Why was the term "supermini" used?To distinguish more powerful minicomputers that approached mainframes in capability. Superminis (such as the DEC VAX or Data General Eclipse MV/8000) were usually 32-bit at a time when most minicomputers (such as the PDP-11 or Data General Eclipse or IBM Series/1) were 16-bit.How did superminis differ from most other minicomputers?Superminis were usually 32-bit, while most other minicomputers were 16-bit. These traditional minicomputers in the last few decades of the 20th century, found in small to medium-sized businesses, laboratories and embedded in (for example) hospital CAT scanners, often would be rack-mounted and connect to one or more terminals or tape/card readers, like mainframes and unlike most personal computers, but require less space and electrical power than a typical mainframe.In what ways were traditional minicomputers like mainframes, and how did they differ?Like mainframes, they were often rack-mounted and connected to terminals or readers, but they needed less space and electrical power. This term has fallen into disuse.[2]What has happened to the term "minicomputer"?It has fallen into disuse.

Mainframe computers

[edit]

The term mainframe computer was created to distinguish the traditional, large, institutional computer intended to service multiple users from the smaller, single-user machines.Why was the term "mainframe computer" created?To distinguish large institutional computers serving many users from smaller single-user machines. These computers are capable of handling and processing very large amounts of data quickly.What are mainframe computers especially good at?Handling and processing very large amounts of data quickly. Mainframe computers are used in large institutions such as government, banks, and large corporations.What kinds of organizations use mainframe computers?Large institutions such as governments, banks, and large corporations. They are measured in MIPS (million instructions per second) and can respond to hundreds of millions of users at a timeHow is the power of a mainframe measured?In MIPS, or million instructions per second.[citation needed].

Supercomputers

[edit]

A supercomputer is focused on performing tasks involving intense numerical calculations such as weather forecasting, fluid dynamics, nuclear simulations, theoretical astrophysics, and complex scientific computations.What kind of work is a supercomputer focused on?Intense numerical calculations, such as weather forecasting, fluid dynamics, and nuclear simulations. A supercomputer is a computer that is at the front-line of current processing capacity, particularly speed of calculation.What makes a computer a supercomputer?Being at the front line of current processing capacity, particularly speed of calculation. The term supercomputer itself is rather fluid, and the speed of today's supercomputers tends to become typical of tomorrow's ordinary computer.Why is the term "supercomputer" described as fluid?Because the speed of today's supercomputers tends to become typical of tomorrow's ordinary computers. Supercomputer processing speeds are measured in floating-point operations per second, or FLOPS.What unit measures supercomputer processing speed?FLOPS, or floating-point operations per second. An example of a floating-point operation is the calculation of mathematical equations in real numbers.What is an example of a floating-point operation?Calculating mathematical equations in real numbers. In terms of computational capability, memory size and speed, I/O technology, and topological issues such as bandwidth and latency, supercomputers are the most powerful, are very expensive, and not cost-effective just to perform batch or transaction processing.Why are supercomputers not used just for batch or transaction processing?They are the most powerful but very expensive, so that use would not be cost-effective.

Classes by function

[edit]

Servers

[edit]

Server usually refers to a computer that is dedicated to providing one or more services.What does the term "server" usually refer to?A computer dedicated to providing one or more services. A server is expected to be reliable (e.g. error-correction of RAM; redundant cooling; self-monitoring, RAID), fit for running for several years, and giving useful diagnosis in case of an error.What qualities are expected of a server?It should be reliable, fit to run for several years, and able to give useful diagnosis when an error occurs. For even increased security, the server may be mirrored.Why might a server be mirrored?For even greater security. Many smaller servers are actually personal computers that have been dedicated to provide services for other computers.How do many smaller servers come into being?Personal computers are dedicated to providing services for other computers.

  • A database server is a server which uses a database application that provides database services to other computer programs or to computers.What does a database server do?It runs a database application that provides database services to other programs or computers. Database management systems (DBMSs) frequently provide database-server functionality, and some database management systems (such as MySQL) rely exclusively on the client–server model for database access while others (such as SQLite) are meant for using as an embedded database.How do MySQL and SQLite differ in the way they are used?MySQL relies exclusively on the client–server model, while SQLite is meant to be used as an embedded database. Users access a database server either through a "front end" running on the user's computer – which displays requested data – or through the "back end", which runs on the server and handles tasks such as data analysis and storage.What are the two ways users access a database server?Through a front end on the user's computer that displays requested data, or through the back end on the server that handles tasks such as analysis and storage.
  • A file server does not normally perform computational tasks or run programs on behalf of its client workstations but manage and store a large collection of computer files.How does the work of a file server differ from that of a typical computer?It does not normally perform computations or run programs for clients; it manages and stores a large collection of files. The crucial function of a file server is storage.What is the crucial function of a file server?Storage. File servers are commonly found in schools and offices, where users use a local area network to connect their client computers and use Network-attached storage (NAS) systems to provide data access.Where are file servers commonly found, and how do users connect to them?In schools and offices, over a local area network, often with network-attached storage providing data access.
  • A web server is a server that can satisfy client requests on the World Wide Web.What is a web server?A server that can satisfy client requests on the World Wide Web. A web server can, in general, contain one or more websites.Can a single web server host more than one website?Yes. It can contain one or more websites. A web server processes incoming network requests over HTTP and several other related protocols.What does a web server do with incoming network requests?It processes them over HTTP and several other related protocols. The primary function of a web server is to store, process and deliver web pages to clients.What is the primary function of a web server?To store, process and deliver web pages to clients. The communication between client and server takes place using the Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP).What protocol do a web client and server use to communicate?HTTP, the Hypertext Transfer Protocol. Pages delivered are most frequently HTML documents, which may include images, style sheets and scripts in addition to the text content.What do web servers most often deliver?HTML documents, which may include images, style sheets and scripts.
  • A terminal server enables organizations to connect devices with an RS-232, RS-422 or RS-485 serial interface to a local area network (LAN).What does a terminal server let organizations do?Connect devices with an RS-232, RS-422 or RS-485 serial interface to a local area network. Products marketed as terminal servers can be very simple devices that do not offer any security functionality, such as data encryption and user authentication.Why might some terminal servers be a security concern?Some are very simple devices with no security features such as data encryption or user authentication. These provide GUI sessions that can be used by client PCs that work someway like a remote control.How do some terminal servers serve client PCs?They provide GUI sessions that work somewhat like a remote control. Only the screen (and audio) output is shown on the client.What part of a terminal-server session is shown on the client?Only the screen and audio output. The GUI applications run on the server, data (like in files) would be stored in the same LAN, thus avoiding problems, should a client PC be damaged or stolen.What advantage comes from running GUI applications on the server and storing data on the same LAN?Problems are avoided if a client PC is damaged or stolen.

A server may run several virtual machines (VMs) for different activities, supplying the same environment to each VM as if it ran on dedicated hardware.What can a server do with virtual machines?Run several VMs for different activities, each with an environment like dedicated hardware. Different operating systems (OS) can therefore be run at the same time.What does virtualization make possible regarding operating systems?Different operating systems can run at the same time. This technology approach needs special hardware support to be useful and was first the domain of mainframes and other large computers.What does this virtualization approach need to be useful, and where did it first appear?It needs special hardware support, and was first the domain of mainframes and other large computers. Nowadays, most personal computers are equipped for this task, but for long-term operation or critical systems, specialized server hardware may be needed.When might specialized server hardware still be needed, even though most PCs support virtualization?For long-term operation or critical systems.
Another approach is to implement VMs on the operating system level, so all VMs run on the same OS instance (or incarnation), but are fundamentally separated to not interfere with each other.How does operating-system-level virtualization differ from other VM approaches?All VMs run on the same OS instance, but are kept separate so they do not interfere with each other.

Workstations

[edit]

Workstations are computers that are intended to serve one user and may contain special hardware enhancements not found on a personal computer.What distinguishes a workstation from an ordinary personal computer?It serves one user and may contain special hardware enhancements not found on a personal computer. By the mid-1990s personal computers reached the processing capabilities of mini computers and workstations.What happened to the gap between personal computers and workstations by the mid-1990s?Personal computers reached the processing capabilities of minicomputers and workstations. Also, with the release of multi-tasking systems such as OS/2, Windows NT and Linux, the operating systems of personal computers could do the job of this class of machines.Why could personal computers do the job of workstations after multi-tasking systems appeared?Operating systems such as OS/2, Windows NT and Linux let personal computers do that class of work. Today, the term is used to describe desktop PCs with high-performance hardware.What does the term "workstation" describe today?Desktop PCs with high-performance hardware. Such hardware is usually aimed at a professional, rather than enthusiast, market (e.g. dual-processor motherboards, error-correcting memory, professional graphics cards).What market is workstation hardware usually aimed at?Professional rather than enthusiast users, with features such as dual-processor motherboards, error-correcting memory, and professional graphics cards.

Information appliances

[edit]

Information appliances are computers specially designed to perform a specific "user-friendly" function—such as editing text, playing music, photography, videography etc.What are information appliances designed to do?Perform a specific user-friendly function, such as editing text, playing music, or photography. The term is most commonly applied to battery-operated mobile devices, though there are also wearable devices.What kind of devices are information appliances most commonly?Battery-operated mobile devices, though wearable devices exist too.

Embedded computers

[edit]

Embedded computers are computers that are a part of a machine or device.What are embedded computers?Computers that are part of a machine or device. Embedded computers generally execute a program that is stored in non-volatile memory and is only intended to operate a specific machine or device.What do embedded computers generally run, and for what purpose?A program stored in non-volatile memory, intended only to operate one specific machine or device. Embedded computers are very common. The majority are microcontrollers. Embedded computers are typically required to operate continuously without being reset or rebooted, and once employed in their task the software usually cannot be modified.What operating requirements are typical of embedded computers?They must run continuously without being reset or rebooted, and their software usually cannot be modified once they are in use. An automobile may contain a number of embedded computers; however, a washing machine or DVD player would contain only one microcontroller.How does the number of embedded computers differ between an automobile and a washing machine?A car may contain several, while a washing machine or DVD player would contain only one microcontroller. Embedded computers are chosen to meet the requirements of the specific application, and most are slower and cheaper than CPUs found in a personal computer.How do embedded computers compare with the CPUs in personal computers?Most are slower and cheaper, chosen to meet the needs of the specific application.

Classes by usage

[edit]

Public computer

[edit]

Public computers are open for public uses, possibly as an Interactive kiosk.What are public computers for?Open public use, possibly as an interactive kiosk. There are many places where one can use them, such as cybercafes, schools and libraries.Where can public computers be found?In places such as cybercafes, schools and libraries.

They are normally fire-walled and restricted to run only their pre-installed softwareWhat restrictions are normally placed on public computers?They are firewalled and restricted to running only their pre-installed software.[citation needed]. The operating system is difficult to change and/or resides on a file server.How is the operating system of a public computer typically set up?It is difficult to change and/or resides on a file server. For example, "thin client" machines in educational establishments may be reset to their original state between classes.How can "thin client" machines in schools be kept in their original state?They may be reset to their original state between classes. Public computers are generally not expected to keep an individual's data files.What is generally not expected of public computers regarding user data?They are not expected to keep an individual's data files.

Personal computer

[edit]

A personal computer has one user who may also be the owner (although the term has also come also mean any computer hardware somewhat like the original IBM PC, irrespective of how it is used).What is a personal computer in terms of its users?A computer with one user, who may also be the owner. This user often may use all hardware resources, has complete access to any part of the computer and has rights to install/remove software.What control does the user of a personal computer typically have?Use of all hardware resources, complete access to any part of the computer, and the right to install and remove software. Personal computers normally store personal files, and often the owner/user is responsible for routine maintenance such as removing unwanted files and virus-scanning.Who is usually responsible for maintaining a personal computer?The owner or user, who handles tasks such as removing unwanted files and virus scanning. Some computers in a business setting are for one user but are also served by staff with protocols to ensure proper maintenance.How does maintenance differ for single-user computers in a business setting?They are also served by staff, with protocols to ensure proper maintenance.

Shared computer

[edit]

These are computers where different people might log on at different times; unlike public computers, they would have usernames and passwords assigned on a long-term basis, with the files they see and the computer's settings adjusted to their particular account.How do shared computers differ from public computers?Users have usernames and passwords assigned on a long-term basis, with their own files and settings. Often the important data files will reside on a central file server, so a person could log onto different computers yet still see the same files.Why can a person log onto different shared computers and still see the same files?Important data files often reside on a central file server. The computer (or workstation) might be a "thin client" or X terminal, otherwise it may have its own disk for some or all system files, but usually will need to be networked to the rest of the system for full functionality.What setups can a shared computer have, and what does it usually need?It may be a thin client or X terminal, or have its own disk for some or all system files, but usually must be networked to the rest of the system for full functionality. Such systems normally require a system administrator to set up and maintain the hardware and software.What do shared-computer systems normally require?A system administrator to set up and maintain the hardware and software.

Display computer

[edit]

Computers that are used just to display selected material (usually audio-visual, or simple slide shows) in a shop, meeting or trade show.What are display computers used for?Showing selected material, usually audio-visual or simple slide shows, in a shop, meeting or trade show. These computers may have more capabilities than they are being used for; they are likely to have WiFi and so be capable of Internet access, but are rarely firewalled (but have restricted port access or monitored in some way).What security trade-off do display computers typically have?They may have WiFi and Internet capability but are rarely firewalled, though they have restricted port access or are monitored in some way. Such computers are used and maintained as appliances, and not normally used as the primary store for important files.How are display computers treated in terms of maintenance and data storage?They are used and maintained as appliances and are not normally the primary store for important files.

Classed by generation of computer technology

[edit]
Main article: History of computing hardware

The history of computing hardware is often used to reference the different generations of computing devices:

  • First generation computers (1940–1955): It used vacuum tubes such as the 6J6[3] or specially designed tubes - or even mechanical arrangements, and were relatively slow, energy-hungry and the earliest computers were less flexible in their programmability.What technology did first-generation computers use, and what were their drawbacks?Vacuum tubes (or even mechanical arrangements); they were slow, energy-hungry, and less flexible in programmability.
  • Second generation computers (1956–1963): It used discrete transistors, and thus smaller and consumes less power.What technology defined second-generation computers, and what benefit did it bring?Discrete transistors, which made them smaller and lower in power consumption.
  • Third generation computers (1964–1970): It used Integrated Circuits (ICs), the main difference between hardware in computers of the 1960s and today being the density of transistors in each IC (beginning with Small Scale Integration chips like the Transistor-transistor logic (TTL) SN7400 gates with 20 transistors, through Medium Scale Integration and Large Scale Integration to Very-large-scale integration (VLSI) with over ten billion transistors in a single silicon-based IC "chip".What is the main difference between 1960s computer hardware and today's?The density of transistors in each integrated circuit.
  • Fourth generation computers (1971–present): It uses Microprocessors, as millions of ICs were built onto a single silicon-based chip.What technology defines fourth-generation computers, and how was it achieved?Microprocessors, built by putting millions of ICs onto a single silicon chip. Since then form factor of computers reduced, task processing & graphic rendering improved and it became more battery-powered with the advent of personal mobile devices such as laptops, tablets, smartphones etc.What changes followed the arrival of fourth-generation computers?Computers shrank in form factor, processing and graphics improved, and battery-powered mobile devices such as laptops, tablets and smartphones appeared.

Classed by tier

[edit]

High performance computing can be described by the scale of its impact:[4]

  • Tier-0: Continent leading
  • Tier-1: National Centers
  • Tier-2: Institutional & Regional Centers
  • Tier-3: Departmental / Local Systems
