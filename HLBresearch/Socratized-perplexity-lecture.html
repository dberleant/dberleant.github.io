Perplexity of a language modelTrue or False: Perplexity summarizes how well a language model assigns probability to the tokens that actually follow.

For a token sequence w1, …, wN, perplexity is the exponential of the average negative log-likelihood per token.

PPL = exp ( − 1 N ∑ i=1 N log P ( wi ∣ w1 , … , wi−1 ) )

  • Perplexity can be a very high numberTrue or False: There is no fixed small upper limit on perplexity; it can grow as predictions become worse.
  • If a "model" gives every word (or token) the same probability of being next:True or False: A predictor that spreads its probability evenly across a large vocabulary will usually give the observed token a small probability.
